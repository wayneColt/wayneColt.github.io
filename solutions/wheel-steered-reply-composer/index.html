Wheel-steered reply composer

The protocol between the wheel and the gate.

By WayneColt · 2026-10-18 · resolves by 2028-04-30 · fabricated:false

An inbound message becomes a reply you steer with a wheel and commit with one press. Nothing leaves until a gate says so.

The mechanism

When a message arrives, the protocol pre-renders every reply it would be willing to send for it: three templates, three decisions under each, five tone bands, three cadences. That is 135 drafts, cached against the message before a hand touches anything. Composing is a scrub, not a typing session.

A wheel or a rocker moves one parameter at a time. One detent is one step, with a hard stop at each end so the hand knows where it is without the eyes. A long press moves to the next parameter. If there is a screen, it shows one monochrome line: AXIS: [TONE] · TONE: Direct Executive (72%) · DRAFTING.

One press on the centre commits. The draft goes to the approval gate, not to the wire. Routine replies pass; a new contact, or anything carrying a deadline, escalates to a person. The device shows the gate's verdict and never says “sent” on its own authority.

No keyboard. No free dictation. The protocol is the product; the wheel is a peripheral. The kit that carries it is called Squishy.

Where the market is

Three physical-control communicators converged in one month (pages read 2026-10-18). Sidephone ships a $249 phone with a magnetic tile bay; founders units have shipped since January 2026. Sprout Pi is an open board that revives a classic click-wheel music player and drives its original wheel and screen; it showed as “coming soon” on 2026-10-15, with no price and no date. Wink is a handset with a swappable bottom bus; reservations opened 2026-10-15.

Each one funds a chassis, a wheel or a bus. None of them ships the composition step: the part between the wheel and the gate. This page is where their users land when they go looking for it.

Three wires

Wire 0 · now · software only
The protocol runs on infrastructure already in service: a compose call returns the rendered draft for four indices; a commit call fires it through the gate. This is the whole value. It needs no new hardware.
Wire 1 · now · the phone already in the pocket
A volume rocker scrubs, a long press switches parameters, one button commits. One number is measured: how often a reply is committed without looking at the screen. That number decides whether a wheel earns a shell.
Wire 2 · when the open click-wheel board lands
A prototype for a hand, not a product. The board scans the real wheel and draws the monochrome line; a small compute module runs the thin client. The shell, the wheel and the driver are the market's. The protocol is ours.
Wire 3 · only if Wire 2 earns it
The same composer as an accessory on someone else's phone.

What this page does not claim

  • That a device exists. There is a kit: a laser-cut plate, a 24-detent encoder with a push switch, a small microcontroller board. Until a working mechanism commits through the real gate, every picture of it is a concept demonstration and every mention of it says so.
  • Any hardware specification. No battery, radio, chipset, latency or signal figure. The prototype's screen carried a latency line; it was a literal in the source and has been removed.
  • Anything about the three products above beyond what their own pages state. The unverified figures that circulate about them are not repeated here.
  • That “reply sent” means sent. It means committed to the gate. The gate decides.
